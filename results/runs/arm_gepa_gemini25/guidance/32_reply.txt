Your primary obstacle is not policy design but syntax, as the vast majority of your proposals are refused for basic formatting errors like invalid YAML or missing `STRUCTURE_DESCRIPTION` and `NEW_WORDS` sections. You must produce syntactically valid candidates to be evaluated. The only marginal success, a tiny improvement from the new word `rdp_and_conf_check`, came from a coordinated change to `victim.bypass_when`, `tables`, and `samplers`, suggesting this is a promising area. In contrast, modifications to `on_fill`, `state`, and `on_hit` have consistently degraded performance and should be avoided. On traces with the most headroom, such as `605.mcf_s` and `619.lbm_s`, the dominant error is `wrong_bypass`, indicating your bypass decisions are too aggressive. Your attempts to fix this with words like `punish_wrong_bypass` have failed because the policy cannot confirm that a miss was caused by a specific past bypass. To learn effectively from `wrong_bypass` mistakes, the policy must remember the identities of recently bypassed lines. The current vocabulary lacks a way to do this, so you should propose a new word that defines a small, signature-based table to log bypassed blocks. This would allow the policy, upon a miss, to check this log and provide direct, accurate negative feedback to its bypass predictor if the missing block is found there.
