Your progress comes from refining `victim.bypass_when` with new condition words. The best design, `ea244a758864`, uses `pred_exceeds_blended_etr` to compare an incoming line's predicted value against an average of resident lines. This averaging is imprecise, leading to many `wrong_bypass` mistakes on `605.mcf_s` and `459.GemsFDTD-1491B` by discarding useful lines, while also causing `kept_dead` errors on `619.lbm_s` and `657.xz_s` by retaining useless ones. A more direct comparison to the victim, as seen in the successful `pred_gt_relaxed_victim_etr`, is a better path. However, to resolve the conflict between bypassing too much and too little, the decision must also consider the confidence of the prediction. The vocabulary lacks a way to express this; a new condition word could allow bypassing only when a prediction is *confidently* worse than the victim's value, which would help reduce both `wrong_bypass` and `kept_dead` mistakes. You must stop submitting proposals with invalid names like `confident_pred_exceeds_victim_etr` or redeclared words like `rdp_and_conf_gt`, as these formatting errors cause many refusals. Furthermore, all attempts to change `procedures.access` and `signatures` have failed and should be abandoned.
